# A third of Nashville's police incident ledger is not crime - and the crime side fell about 10 percent for the second straight window

One line: of the 945,811 records in the Metro Nashville Police Department's incident ledger since 2019, 34.0 percent sit under MNPD's own non-criminal incident-type codes - police inquiries, found and lost property, transports - while records under criminal offense codes fell 9.9 percent in Jan-Oct 2025 and another 10.1 percent in Jan-Oct 2026, a decline carried by vehicle break-ins, vehicle theft and robbery, with assaults flat at their 2019 level.

## Source and definitions

- Source: Metro Nashville Police Department Incidents layer (ArcGIS FeatureServer service Metro_Nashville_Police_Department_Incidents_view, layer 0), pulled in full on 2026-11-04: 945,811 records, 783,832 distinct incidents, offenses occurred 2019-01-01 to 2026-11-03. The agency's own metadata document (MNPD Open Data metadata v2.0, 2023-06-13) was read before analysis and is archived with this package.
- Unit: one row is one offense-victim record within an incident, per the metadata; an incident carries 1.21 records on average. Every figure in this brief is a record count unless it says incidents.
- Code classes: the metadata's Appendix D lists the FBI's NIBRS offense codes (09A to 90Z) and then appends MNPD's local incident-type series (610-850): matter of record, found property, lost property, recovered property, non-criminal deaths, accidental overdoses, transports and similar. This brief calls the first group criminal offense codes (623,874 records, 66.0 percent) and the second local incident-type codes (321,859 records, 34.0 percent); 78 records carry no code. Six codes inside the local series name criminal conduct (64A, 64B, 700, 710, 730, 850; 3,553 records in total). Reclassifying them as criminal moves the Jan-Oct 2025 to 2026 criminal change from -10.1 percent to -10.0 percent, so the split follows Appendix D's own structure and the sensitivity is printed.
- Grouping is by NIBRS code throughout. The served Offense_Description field is the department's RMS/Tennessee label set, not the NIBRS label: 44 of the 72 codes carry more than one description, and code 23F (NIBRS: Theft From Motor Vehicle) is described as "BURGLARY- MOTOR VEHICLE" on 71,995 records. Labels in this brief are the Appendix D NIBRS labels.
- Time: Incident_Occurred, local time (America/Chicago). Incident_Number is not used for time: its year prefix disagrees with the occurred year on 15,313 records (1.6 percent). Full years 2019-2025 are complete; 2026 is partial and is used only in matched Jan-Oct windows. Those windows are themselves slightly understated at the tail: the Oct 1-19 versus Sep 14-30 daily-rate ratio is 0.928 for 2026 against 0.985-1.018 for 2023-2025, which implies roughly 400 records (about 0.5 percent of the Jan-Oct 2026 window) had not yet been reported at the pull. Jan-Oct 2026 figures are low by about that amount; it does not change any direction or ranking in this brief.
- Geography: the layer's Zone and RPA patrol fields are populated through 2021 and then stop (known on 96.5 percent of 2019 records, 90.2 percent of 2021, 17.7 percent of 2022, and effectively none from 2023 on). Precinct in this brief is therefore spatial: each record's privacy-rounded coordinates are placed in the nine official precinct polygons. On the 352,229 records where both exist, the spatial precinct agrees with the precinct derived from the record's own zone code on 96.4 percent; the disagreements concentrate on the South / Midtown Hills border, the expected signature of coordinates rounded to three decimals. ZIP codes are not used for geography: the field is missing on 46.6 percent of records in every year.

## Findings

**F1 - A third of the ledger is not criminal offenses, and that share is growing.**
321,859 of 945,811 records (34.0 percent) sit under local incident-type codes. One code dominates: 740 (Matter of Record) holds 205,180 records, 21.7 percent of the entire ledger, and carries exactly two descriptions - POLICE INQUIRY (185,355) and TRANSPORT (19,825). The local share of each full year rose from 31.8 percent in 2019 to 36.1 percent in 2025, and stands at 38.5 percent of Jan-Oct 2026 records. In full years, local-code records grew 12.3 percent from 2019 to 2025 (38,098 to 42,775) while criminal-code records fell.

**F2 - The headline decline is entirely on the criminal side, and it has repeated.**
Total records fell 8.7 percent between full years 2023 and 2025 (129,728 to 118,421). Criminal-code records fell 11.7 percent over the same span (85,651 to 75,641) and stand 7.3 percent below their 2019 level. In matched Jan-Oct windows the criminal decline has now repeated at the same size twice: 71,454 records (2023) to 63,634 (2025), -9.9 percent from 2024 to 2025, then 57,222 (2026), -10.1 percent. Local-code records in the same windows went the other way or held: 36,925 (Jan-Oct 2023), 36,005 (2025), 35,783 (2026).

**F3 - The criminal decline is concentrated in vehicle and street property categories; assaults did not fall.**
Jan-Oct windows, 2019 to 2026 unless noted. The vehicle cluster (240 Motor Vehicle Theft + 23F Theft From Motor Vehicle + 23G Theft of Motor Vehicle Parts) fell from 16,076 records in Jan-Oct 2023 to 8,585 in Jan-Oct 2026 (-46.6 percent), and sits 29.0 percent below its 2019 window. Its parts: 23F fell from 9,902 (2023) to 4,910 (-50.4 percent); 240 fell from 4,667 to 2,753 (-41.0 percent), though 240 remains 6.3 percent above its 2019 window. Robbery (120) fell from 2,230 in Jan-Oct 2019 to 640 (-71.3 percent; full years 2,705 to 1,050, -61.2 percent). Vandalism (290) and burglary (220) each fell 30.9 percent against their 2019 windows. Against that: simple assault (13B) is flat, 8,851 to 8,772 (-0.9 percent), and aggravated assault (13A) rose to a 5,170 peak in Jan-Oct 2023 and returned to 3,701, essentially its 2019 level (3,720, -0.5 percent). Intimidation (13C) rose 36.1 percent (3,086 to 4,199). Drug violations rose against 2023: 35A from 2,894 to 3,365 (+16.3 percent) and 35B from 1,946 to 2,311 (+18.8 percent), though 35A remains below its 2019 window (3,814). One measurement caution attaches to 23F: January 2025 alone holds 1,648 records against 669-1,155 in every other January, an unexplained one-month surge that the report-lag test says is not a backfill artifact (1.15 percent of the month's records were reported more than 30 days after occurrence, against a 1.07 percent baseline for the code). Excluding January, Jan-Oct 2025 23F falls to 7,207. No month-level claim in this brief rests on it.

**F4 - The ledger's 31.7 percent UNFOUNDED rate is a composition artifact, not a clearance measure.**
Across all records, incident status at the pull date reads OPEN on 46.4 percent, UNFOUNDED on 31.7 percent (299,992 records), CLEARED BY ARREST on 15.0 percent, and REFUSED TO COOPERATE on 6.1 percent. Split by code class the picture inverts: 96.3 percent of all unfounded records (289,016 of 299,992) sit under local incident-type codes, where the unfounded share is 89.8 percent - the routine closing status for an inquiry, a found-property log or a transport. Within criminal offense codes the unfounded share is 1.8 percent (10,971 of 623,874), open is 66.9 percent, and cleared by arrest is 21.1 percent. The criminal-code unfounded share was stable at 1.6-1.9 percent across full years 2019-2025; the 2.4 percent shown for partial 2026 is a censoring artifact, because status is a snapshot at pull time and recent incidents have had less time to resolve. For the same reason, no year's OPEN share is compared here as performance. Status is recorded at incident level in the source: all 783,832 incidents carry exactly one status across their records (verified).

**F5 - Offense geography and crash geography are near-mirror images across the nine precincts.**
For 2019-2025, criminal offense records (spatial precinct, known on 563,734 records) and reported crashes (recorded precinct, known on 178,685) distribute across precincts with a rank correlation of 0.80. No precinct's share differs by more than 2.3 points between the two ledgers. The gaps that exist: North takes 12.7 percent of offense records against 10.3 percent of crashes (+2.3 points) and East 10.5 against 8.3 (+2.2), while Midtown Hills takes 13.1 percent of crashes against 11.5 percent of offense records (-1.6) and South 14.2 against 12.9 (-1.2). Southeast (13.0 / 13.2), Hermitage (12.4 / 12.6), Madison (10.1 / 9.6), West (8.6 / 9.7) and Central (8.5 / 9.0) sit close in both. Method control: recomputing crash shares spatially instead of from the recorded precinct moves no precinct's share by more than 1.4 points, so the comparison is not an artifact of mixing assignment methods. These are shares of each ledger's records - counts, not risk: no population, traffic-volume or exposure denominator is applied, and none is published in this brief.

**F6 - The calls ledger completes the triad - and its geography is the outlier.**
MNPD's calls-for-service layer for 2025 (486,611 calls, deposited 2026-10-16) joins to this ledger on the complaint number the two systems share. Of 72,041 distinct complaint numbers carried on 2025 calls, 68,373 (94.9 percent) match an incident in this layer, and 66,470 match an incident that occurred in 2025 - which is 67.2 percent of the 98,930 incidents that occurred in 2025. The other side of the funnel is thinly documented: only 15.2 percent of 2025 calls carry a complaint number at all, so the join describes the documented minority of calls, not all demand. Geographically, the three ledgers agree on incidents and crashes (2025 rank correlation 0.82) but calls diverge (rank correlation against incidents 0.32). Midtown Hills is the largest call precinct (13.5 percent of known-precinct calls) but mid-pack for incident records (10.2 percent) and crashes (11.8 percent). Southeast reverses: 10.8 percent of calls but 13.7 percent of incident records and 16.7 percent of crashes. North is incident-heavy (12.4 percent) against calls (10.4) and crashes (10.1). The denominators differ in completeness and the brief prints them: precinct is unknown for 23.1 percent of calls, 2.4 percent of incident records and 2.4 percent of crashes in 2025.

Context figures, stated once and not carried into findings: 21.1 percent of all records are flagged domestic-related, and 48.0 percent of aggravated and simple assault records (13A + 13B) carry the flag; the all-records share moves between 19.5 and 23.0 percent by year. Report type is dominated by dispatched calls (D, 770,232 records); self-initiated (S) holds 96,666, teleserve (T) 60,910, walk-in (W) 10,253. The teleserve share of each year moved between 4.6 and 9.9 percent with no trend break (7.8 percent in 2019, 4.8 percent in 2025 and 2026), so the criminal decline is not a visible shift of intake channel.

## What this might mean

This section is interpretation. The findings above are measured counts; the readings below are one way to read them, each paired with the strongest alternative the data cannot rule out.

- Reading: Nashville's reported-crime workload and its non-crime service workload are decoupling - offense records fell about a tenth in each of the last two matched windows while the inquiry-and-property ledger held or grew, so a single "incidents" total now mostly measures the mix, not either workload. Alternative: recording practice, not workload, is what moved - if officers log inquiries more completely than before, the local share rises without any change in underlying demand; the layer records what was logged, and this brief cannot separate the two.
- Reading: the offense decline is real but narrow - it is a vehicle-and-street-property story (vehicle cluster -46.6 percent against 2023, robbery -71.3 percent against 2019) while person offenses inside homes and relationships did not fall (simple assault flat, aggravated assault back to its 2019 level, intimidation up 36.1 percent, and 48.0 percent of assault records domestic-flagged). Alternative: vehicle categories are the most reporting-sensitive - break-ins and thefts reach the ledger only when a victim files, so part of the vehicle drop could be reporting fatigue rather than fewer events; the assault flatness argues against a blanket reporting collapse, but the data here cannot price the reporting component.
- Reading: anyone quoting the ledger's 31.7 percent unfounded rate as a performance figure is quoting the inquiry pile. Alternative: even within criminal codes, status mixes investigative outcomes with administrative closures, so the 1.8 percent criminal unfounded share should be read as a bookkeeping rate, not a verdict on report quality in either direction.
- Reading: patrol and prevention geography can be planned off one map for offenses and crashes (rank correlation 0.80-0.82), but staffing-to-demand cannot: calls peak in Midtown Hills and Central in a way neither outcome ledger matches. Alternative: the calls divergence may be an artifact of the calls layer itself - 23.1 percent of calls have no determinable precinct and only 15.2 percent carry the complaint number that anchors the join - so the safest claim is that the documented parts of the three ledgers diverge, with the divergence's size bounded by those missing shares.

## Who can act

- **MNPD command staff and the Metro Council public safety committee (lever: how the ledger is quoted and resourced).** The Metro Council meets in special session on Thursday, November 5, 2026; any figure quoted there from this ledger should be split by code class before it is quoted at all, because the combined total (down 8.7 percent, 2023 to 2025) understates the criminal-side decline (down 11.7 percent) and buries the non-criminal workload that drives much of the department's service demand. Cost class: none, a reporting convention. Outcome measure: budget and staffing documents that cite the two code classes separately, checkable against the next pull of this layer.
- **MNPD Open Data and RMS export owners (lever: the export itself).** Three concrete repairs, all in the publisher's control: restore or document the Zone and RPA fields, populated through 2021 and empty since 2023, which removes the patrol-geography dimension from every public use of this layer; align the served Report_Type_Description values with the metadata's Appendix A (the service currently labels S as SUSPECT where the metadata says SELF-INITIATED, W as WITNESS where the metadata says WALK-IN, and serves no description at all for T, O and CIR on 68,660 records); and document the January 2025 code-23F surge (1,648 records, roughly double any other January). Cost class: staff time on the export job. Outcome measure: a re-pull in which Zone/RPA are populated for current records and the description fields match Appendix A, verifiable against Appendix A of the archived metadata.
- **MNPD and Metro ITS (lever: the calls-to-incidents link).** Two-thirds of 2025 incidents (67.2 percent) link to a call through the shared complaint number, but 84.8 percent of calls carry no complaint number, so the demand-to-outcome funnel cannot be audited for most calls. Populating the complaint number on every call record that generates an incident is a data-entry and CAD configuration change, not a new system. Cost class: low, configuration and QA. Outcome measure: the share of calls carrying a complaint number in the next annual calls layer, against the 15.2 percent measured here.
- **Metro Council and the Department of Transportation (lever: joint offense-and-crash geography).** F5's near-mirror precinct shares (rank correlation 0.80) mean precinct-level safety planning does not need two maps for offenses and crashes; North and East are the two precincts where the offense share runs ahead of the crash share, and Southeast is where crashes concentrate most (16.7 percent of 2025 crashes, the highest single-ledger share in the triad). Cost class: none to adopt the shared framing; program costs sit in existing Vision Zero and precinct prevention budgets. Outcome measure: precinct shares recomputed on the next annual pulls of both layers, published with the same spatial method so the figures stay comparable.

## Data notes

- Primary_Key is not unique, despite the metadata calling it a unique identifier for each record: the key concatenates incident, offense and victim numbers without a separator, and 114 keys are shared by 263 rows carrying distinct OBJECTIDs (149 excess rows). OBJECTID is the row key used here. No deduplication was performed; all counts are as served.
- 27,832 records (2.9 percent) carry an exactly-midnight occurred time, a placeholder pattern; no time-of-day figure is published in this brief. 1,739 records show a reported time before the occurred time; the median report lag is 0.13 days. Incident_Reported reaches back to 1995 in a junk tail.
- The weapon field mixes two vocabularies (NONE / PERSONAL (HANDS) / HANDGUN alongside Unarmed / CLUB) and is empty on 50,754 records; no weapon figure is published.
- Coordinates are empty on 11,762 records and exactly 0,0 on 185 more (11,947 unknown, 1.3 percent); 21 records with coordinates fall outside the Davidson County bounding box and are excluded from spatial assignment. All served coordinates are rounded to exactly three decimals, per the metadata's privacy rounding.
- Victim_Type carries keystroke garbage on 109 rows (values such as "NNNNNN"); those rows are mapped to unknown and no victim-type figure is published.
- One code is served in lowercase: "13c" on 21 records. It is counted in the criminal class total; the 13C category line in this brief counts the 34,713 uppercase records, so intimidation is understated by 21 records (0.06 percent).
- Report type CIR (1,831 records) does not appear in the metadata's Appendix A; it is reported here under its code only.
- The calls layer used for F6 is the 2025 calls-for-service deposit in the Machine Readable Nashville stack (snapshot 2026-10-16; 486,611 calls). Its precinct field is as recorded or zone-derived by that deposit's method, and its own spatial control is weaker than this layer's; the triad comparison prints each ledger's unknown-precinct share alongside its findings so the reader can judge the joins.
- The crash layer used for F5 and F6 is the deposited Nashville Traffic Accidents layer (snapshot 2026-11-01; 222,903 crashes, 2019-2026), the same layer behind the published Nashville traffic accidents brief.
- All findings use records as the unit. Incident-unit figures appear only where stated (the status verification and the 2025 calls join), because status and complaint linkage are incident-level facts in the source.
